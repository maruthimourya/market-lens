# Investing Concepts

Source: Investor.gov — U.S. SEC Office of Investor Education and Advocacy (public domain)
URL: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/investing-basics/glossary

## Price-to-Earnings (P/E) Ratio
A way of gauging whether a stock price is high or low compared to the past or to
other companies. It divides the current stock price by a per-share earnings figure.

## Stock
An instrument that signifies an ownership position (called equity) in a corporation.
Each share gives a claim on a proportional part of the company's assets and profits.

## Market Capitalization
The company's total market value, found by multiplying the current public market
price of one share by the number of outstanding shares.

## Earnings Per Share (EPS)
A public company's net profit divided by the number of its common shares.

## Dividend
A portion of a company's profit paid to shareholders. Companies that pay dividends
often do so on a regular schedule, though they can pay at any time.

## Capital Gain
The profit that comes when an investment is sold for more than the price the
investor paid for it.

## Bond
A debt security, similar to an IOU. When you buy a bond, you lend money to the
issuer in exchange for repayment of the amount borrowed plus interest.

## Mutual Fund
An SEC-registered open-end investment company that pools money from many investors
and invests it in stocks, bonds, and other securities.

## ETF (Exchange-Traded Fund)
An exchange-traded investment product that must register with the SEC as an open-end
investment company. ETFs trade on exchanges like stocks.

## Index
A measure of performance for a specific "basket" of stocks that represents a market
or sector. The Dow Jones Industrial Average is one example.

## Diversification
Spreading your money across different investments so one poor performer has less
effect on your overall results. In short: don't put all your eggs in one basket.

## Dollar Cost Averaging
Investing your money in equal portions, at regular intervals, regardless of the ups
and downs in the market. This can help manage risk over time.

## Risk
The degree of uncertainty about the rate of return on an asset, including the
potential harm if financial returns differ from what the investor expected.

## Portfolio
The combined holdings of stock, bond, commodity, real estate, and other investments
of an individual or institutional investor.

## Bull Market
A time when stock prices are rising and market sentiment is optimistic. Generally, a
broad index rises 20% or more over at least two months.

## Bear Market
A time when stock prices are declining and market sentiment is pessimistic.
Generally, a broad index falls 20% or more over at least two months.
